# Published form images are Masked Renditions; the Original is never altered

Publicly published Statutory Form images are **Masked Renditions**: derived
copies with `ID/Passport No.`, `Tel. Contact` and the free-text
`Reasons for Refusal to Sign` obscured. Names, signature marks and the
signed/refused pattern remain visible. A Masked Rendition carries its own hash,
is stored beside the Original, and never replaces it. Candidates and petition
exports receive the Original.

## Why mask these fields and not others

Form 34A's agents table carries `Name of Candidate or Agent`, `ID/Passport No.`,
`Party Name/Independent Candidate`, `Tel. Contact`, `Signature` and `Date`.
Publishing it unmasked would republish a national ID number and a telephone
number for every Party Agent who signed, at tens of thousands of stations, in a
searchable form. Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 binds us as an ordinary data
controller; the fact that Reg 79(2A)(d) requires the form to be posted at the
polling station, and that IEBC itself published all 46,229 forms in 2022, is
not a defence available to us.

Masking the whole block was considered and rejected. The signature block is the
most probative part of the image — it is precisely what both Supreme Court
Registrars examined in 2017 and 2022 — and a reader must be able to see *that*
three agents signed and one refused.

## Consequences

- Photographed forms sit at angles, so automatic masking can miss. **A person
  confirms each mask during review.**
- Where mask confidence is low, publish the counts with "image under review".
  **Never default to publishing an unmasked form.**
- This is the first question in the pre-launch legal review.
